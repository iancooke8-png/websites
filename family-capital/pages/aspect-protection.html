Protection

(because one event can unwind decades of planning)

Life cover, critical illness, key person insurance. Not the most exciting conversation — but without it, a single event can destroy decades of careful work.

Critical illness is our biggest claim area.

It's not something people like to think about. But serious illness doesn't wait until your finances are in order. Critical illness cover provides a lump sum when you need it most — and for families, it's not optional. It's essential.

And when it pays out, it needs to go to the right place. Which is why every policy we arrange goes into trust.

Every policy in trust. Every single time.

Without a trust, life cover proceeds go through probate — potentially into the estate, potentially subject to inheritance tax. With a trust, they go exactly where you want them, when you want them. There's no reason not to do this. We do it as standard.

Protection and borrowing go hand in hand.

If you have a mortgage, the debt doesn't disappear when you do. Life cover and critical illness cover linked to your borrowing means your family isn't left with the burden. This connects directly to Step 1 — mortgages.

Relevant Life — tax-efficient cover through your business.

If you're a business owner, your company can pay for your personal life cover and get tax relief on the premiums. It's called a Relevant Life policy. The company pays, gets corporation tax relief, and you get cover without it being a benefit in kind. It's one of the most efficient ways to arrange protection — and it's often overlooked.

Made a large gift? You might need cover for seven years.

When you make a significant gift — to family, into a trust, or to reduce your estate — there's a potential inheritance tax liability that tapers over seven years. A Gift Inter Vivos policy covers that liability while it exists. It's a decreasing term policy that matches the taper. Simple, effective, and often forgotten.

What happens to the business if a key person dies?

Share purchase agreements, key person cover, partnership protection. If a business partner dies, who buys their share? How does the business survive the loss of a key individual? These aren't hypothetical questions — they need answers and policies in place before they're needed.

How this fits into your wider plan

Protection doesn't exist in isolation — it connects to your borrowing, your trusts, your tax planning, and your family's future.
