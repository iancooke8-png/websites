Mortgages

(and everything else you owe)

Before we decide where your money should go, we look at what it's already doing.

There's a certain snobbery in wealth management where firms will say: "We don't do mortgages — we focus on investments." Which is fine… until you realise: You can't make sensible investment decisions without understanding the debt behind them.

You don't invest in isolation. You invest around your debt.

Before we talk about portfolios, tax wrappers or long-term returns, we start with a simple question: What do you owe — and what might you owe in the future?

Because every financial decision you make sits on top of that foundation.

Not all debt is bad. But all debt needs thinking about.

High-interest credit cards

Expensive personal loans

These get cleared first. No debate.

Mortgages (typically the cheapest borrowing you'll access)

Business or qualifying debt (which may carry tax advantages)

Debt itself isn't the issue. How it fits into your wider plan is.

This is where most people get it wrong.

It's very easy to separate the conversation: 'Here's my mortgage' / 'Here are my investments' — But in reality, they are directly linked.

If you're investing cautiously while paying interest on a loan: There are plenty of situations where your investment returns — after tax — won't outperform your borrowing cost.

Which leads to a slightly uncomfortable conclusion: You may be better off reducing the debt first. Not always. But often enough that it should never be ignored.

What we look at:

  • Interest rates vs expected returns
  • Tax on income and withdrawals
  • Cashflow and flexibility
  • Your overall risk position

There isn't a default answer — but there is a right answer for you.

  • Mortgage interest is paid from post-tax income
  • Investment returns may be: taxed as income, taxed as capital gains, or sheltered (depending on wrappers)
  • Lower-risk portfolios may not exceed borrowing costs
  • Overpaying debt reduces flexibility
  • Investing introduces volatility

The decision isn't just about return — it's about risk, tax, and control.

The biggest financial wins are often the least exciting.

We're not always talking about dramatic changes. Sometimes it's: Refinancing a mortgage / Adjusting repayment strategy / Restructuring existing borrowing.

But: If you reduce what you're paying out, you increase what you can invest. And over time, that matters far more than people expect.

If money has moved, it counts — even if it's informal.

A proper plan doesn't stop at banks and lenders. We also look at: Money you've lent to family / Support you may give in the future / Debts owed to you.

Even when it's not written down, it still affects your financial position — and your options.

Paying off a mortgage feels incredible.

There's no denying it — it's one of the most satisfying financial milestones there is. A real sense of freedom.

But: That doesn't automatically mean it was the most efficient financial decision. Sometimes it is. Sometimes keeping the debt and deploying capital elsewhere makes more sense.

This is where logic and emotion need to be balanced — not confused.

Debt isn't a separate conversation. It drives everything.

Every decision here affects the rest of your plan. This is why we deal with it first.
